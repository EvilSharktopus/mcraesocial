SOCIAL 10-1 — ECONOMIC GLOBALIZATION JIGSAWDeveloper Handoff DocumentDeveloper HandoffEconomic Globalization Jigsaw — submit.mcraesocial.comOVERVIEWStack: React (Vite) + Firebase (Firestore + Auth) + VercelPaste protection: replicate dual-layer logic from SubmissionPage.jsx (onPaste/onDrop/onDragOver props + native capture listener via useEffect)Auth: existing student login system (not Google sign-in)What This BuildsA two-phase collaborative research tool for a class of 30 students. Students are divided into 6 topic groups of 5. Each student owns one subtopic within their group, researches it individually, and submits to a shared real-time pool visible to their whole group. Groups then use the combined pool to answer synthesis questions together.Phase 2 (next class, teacher-facilitated) is offline — students deliver findings verbally from memory. The platform only needs to support Phase 1.User FlowsFlow A — First time entering the activity1  Student logs in with existing credentials → redirected to activity landing page2  Landing page shows 6 topic cards with current enrollment counts (e.g. "3/5 spots taken")3  Student selects a topic — if < 5 students enrolled, they proceed; if full, button is disabled4  Student selects their subtopic (1–5) — first-come first-served, taken slots shown as disabled5  Student is locked into their topic + subtopic for the session (stored in Firestore)6  Student lands on their Research PageFlow B — Research Page•  Shows their assigned subtopic question + 2–3 guiding research prompts•  Paste-protected textarea for their research response (replicate SubmissionPage.jsx logic)•  Optional: image upload field (single image, stored in Firebase Storage)•  Live sidebar/panel showing other group members' submissions as they come in (Firestore real-time listener)•  Once all 5 subtopics are submitted, group unlocks the Synthesis Questions sectionFlow C — Synthesis Questions•  3 synthesis questions per topic (see content section below)•  Any group member can type the group's answer — one shared response field per question•  Paste-protected (same logic)•  Submit button sends everything to Firestore and marks the group's activity as completeFlow D — Returning student (already assigned)•  On login, check Firestore for existing assignment → skip topic/subtopic selection entirely•  Drop straight into Research Page with prior work pre-populated if already submittedFirestore Data ModelCollection: jigsawActivitiesOne document per activity instance. Do NOT rely on client-side count checks — race conditions will cause over-enrollment.Same pattern for subtopic claiming — transaction checks takenBy === null before writing userId.Real-time group poolUse onSnapshot listener on the submissions subcollection filtered by topicId. This lets students see each other's research appear live without refreshing. Unsubscribe on component unmount.Synthesis unlockSynthesis questions unlock when all 5 submissions in a topic have isSubmitted === true. Check this client-side via the onSnapshot listener — when submissions array length === 5 and all isSubmitted, render the synthesis section.Paste protectionReplicate dual-layer logic from SubmissionPage.jsx on every textarea in this feature:• React props: onPaste, onDrop, onDragOver → all call e.preventDefault() + e.stopPropagation()• Native listener: useEffect attaches addEventListener('paste', handler, { capture: true }) on the DOM element, cleaned up on unmountConsider extracting this into a usePasteBlock(ref) custom hook or a <PasteBlockedTextarea> wrapper component so it doesn't need to be copy-pasted into every instance.Suggested Component StructureComponentResponsibilityJigsawLandingShow 6 topic cards with enrollment counts. Guiding prompts are shown to the student on their research page to scaffold their search.T1WWI and Economic FalloutS1: What did the war cost Canada? (debt, income tax, pensions)S2: What was the Treaty of Versailles and what did it demand of Germany?S3: What were reparations and why did economists like Keynes object?S4: How did WWI disrupt global trade and production?S5: What long-term economic structures did WWI create or accelerate?Synthesis questions:1. How did WWI change who paid for government — and why did that matter long-term?2. Why did the Treaty of Versailles create economic problems rather than solve them?3. What economic lesson from WWI do you think world leaders ignored — and what happened because they ignored it?T2Communist Economics as an AlternativeS1: What economic conditions led to the Russian Revolution?S2: What did a communist economy actually look like in theory?S3: How did Stalin transform the Soviet economy in practice?S4: What was the human cost of forced industrialization?S5: How did the Soviet model challenge Western capitalism as an idea?Synthesis questions:1. What problems with capitalism was communism trying to solve?2. What's the difference between communist theory and what Stalin actually built?3. Why did the existence of the Soviet Union matter to Western countries, even if they opposed communism?T3The Great DepressionS1: How does the stock market work and what caused the crash?S2: What were tariffs and what happened when countries started using them?S3: How did the Depression spread globally — why couldn't countries fix it alone?S4: What happened to Canada specifically — unemployment, prairies, exports?S5: How does the Depression connect to today? (trade wars, tariffs, economic nationalism)Synthesis questions:1. Why did countries raising tariffs make the Depression worse rather than better?2. Why was Canada more exposed to the Depression than some other countries?3. What similarities do you see between the 1930s tariff situation and trade conflicts happening today?T4From Depression to WWII: Government Spending as Economic ToolS1: How did Depression conditions in Germany create the economic appeal of fascism?S2: What did Hitler promise economically and how did he deliver it initially?S3: How did WWII spending pull countries out of the Depression?S4: What did WWII prove about the role of government in the economy? (Keynesian economics — simply)S5: What was the economic cost of WWII and how did it set up the post-war order?Synthesis questions:1. Why do economic crises sometimes lead people to support extreme political movements?2. What did WWII accidentally prove about how governments can manage economies?3. Is it a problem that it took a world war to end the Depression? What does that suggest about how economies work?T5Bretton WoodsS1: What was Bretton Woods and why did it happen when it did?S2: What is the World Bank and what was it created to do?S3: What is the IMF and what was it created to do?S4: How did the USD become the world's reserve currency and why does that matter?S5: Who was at Bretton Woods — and who wasn't?Synthesis questions:1. Why did the world need a new economic system after WWII — what had failed before?2. What does it mean that colonized nations had no seat at Bretton Woods?3. Who had the most power at Bretton Woods and how does that still shape the global economy today?T6IMF, World Bank, and the Global SouthS1: How do IMF and World Bank loans actually work?S2: What are structural adjustment conditions and what do they typically require?S3: What criticisms have been made of IMF and World Bank loan conditions — and by whom?S4: Who benefits from the current global economic system and who doesn't?S5: How does this connect back to identity (RI 1) — whose voices shaped the global economy?Synthesis questions:1. If a country is desperate for money, is it truly free to refuse IMF conditions? Explain.2. How does the IMF/World Bank dynamic connect to the colonial relationships we studied in RI 1 and RI 2?3. What would a "fair" global economic institution actually look like — who would need to be at the table?Phase 2 — Teacher Facilitated (No Build Required)The following day, teacher returns and runs the oral jigsaw. Students are regrouped so each new group has one representative from each of the 6 topics. Each student delivers their group's findings verbally — 3–5 sentences, no notes, no devices.The platform does not need to support Phase 2. Teacher may want read-only access to all submissions for assessment purposes — consider a simple teacher dashboard view (filtered by activityId, showing all submissions) as a low-priority addition.Social 10-1 — Economic Globalization Jigsaw  ·  Developer Handoff  ·  submit.mcraesocial.com
